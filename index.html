Important health information

This calculator is intended for adults aged 20 and above. BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis or a complete assessment of your health. It does not account for muscle mass, body composition, pregnancy, ethnicity or where fat is stored. Children and teenagers should use age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles instead. Please speak to a qualified healthcare professional about your weight or health.

Understanding BMI

What the number means, how it is worked out, and where it falls short.

?

What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple number that compares your weight to your height. It is widely used by health services as a quick, low-cost way to screen adults for weight ranges that may be linked to health risks.

÷

How is BMI calculated?

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

Example: 70 kg and 175 cm (1.75 m) → 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9.

≡

What do the categories mean?

  • Underweight (< 18.5): may point to undernutrition or another health issue.
  • Healthy weight (18.5–24.9): associated with the lowest weight-related risk for most adults.
  • Overweight (25.0–29.9): linked to a raised risk of some conditions.
  • Obesity (30.0+): linked to a higher risk of conditions such as type 2 diabetes and heart disease.
±

Advantages and limitations of BMI

Advantages

  • Quick, free and needs only a scale and tape measure.
  • Easy to repeat and track over time.
  • Useful for comparing groups in population health studies.
  • A helpful starting point for a conversation with a clinician.

Limitations

  • Cannot tell muscle from fat — athletes may score as "overweight".
  • Ignores where fat is stored; waist size can matter more.
  • May over- or under-estimate risk in older adults and some ethnic groups.
  • Not suitable for pregnancy, children or teenagers.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions people ask most about BMI.

Is BMI accurate?

BMI is a reasonable screening tool for most adults, but it is an estimate. Because it only uses height and weight, it can misclassify people with a lot of muscle, older adults who have lost muscle, and people of different body shapes. Treat it as one piece of information, not a verdict.

Why is this calculator only for adults aged 20 and over?

Children and teenagers are still growing, and healthy body fat changes with age and sex. For people under 20, BMI is interpreted using age- and sex-specific growth charts (BMI-for-age percentiles) rather than the fixed adult categories used here.

Are the categories the same for men and women?

Yes. The standard adult BMI categories are the same for all adults, regardless of sex. However, women tend to carry more body fat than men at the same BMI, which is one reason BMI should be interpreted alongside other measures.

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is described as a healthy weight range. Some health organisations recommend lower thresholds for certain ethnic groups, such as people of South Asian, Chinese or Black African or Caribbean heritage, because risk can rise at a lower BMI.

What else can I measure besides BMI?

Waist circumference and waist-to-height ratio are simple extra checks for fat around the middle, which is closely linked to health risk. A healthcare professional may also consider blood pressure, blood tests, family history, activity levels and diet.

My BMI is outside the healthy range. What should I do?

Don't panic — BMI alone does not diagnose anything. If you're concerned, book an appointment with your doctor or another qualified healthcare professional, who can look at your overall health and give advice that suits you.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. All calculations run in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server or saved.
